Alaskan Earthquakes Hypocenters

A Three-Year Analysis of the Wadati-Benioff Zone

Near Alaska, the Pacific Plate moves northwestward relative to the North American Plate at a rate of approximately 5–7 centimeters per year. As it moves, it subducts (dives beneath) the North American Plate because oceanic crust is denser than continental crust.

In this study, I mapped earthquakes of magnitude 3.5 or higher within a geographic bounding box of 54°N to 62°N latitude and 142°W to 158°W longitude, encompassing Anchorage, the Kenai Peninsula, and Kodiak Island. This region covers the Alaska-Aleutian Megathrust, where seismicity occurs both on the shallow plate interface and deep within the subducting Pacific slab. Each seismic event is represented by a circle, where the size of the circle is proportional to the earthquake's magnitude.

The data span three years (2023–2026), revealing that earthquake hypocenters become progressively deeper as they move inland toward the north/northwest. This depth is visualized through a color gradient that begins with pale yellow (shallow earthquakes), transitions through bright orange (moderate earthquakes) and vivid red (deep earthquakes), and concludes with dark red for the deepest seismic events. To maintain geographical context at all scales, the visualization utilizes zoom-dependent scaling. This ensures that circle sizes remain consistent relative to the terrain as you zoom in, accurately representing the physical extent of the seismic activity.
